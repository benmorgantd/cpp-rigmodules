# Decisions: Modules

The module nodes and how they are designed: two-bone IK, Ribbon, Limb, control placement, template keys, module naming, and what comes after the limb. Part of the decision log: the Index and the open items are in [`../decisions.md`](../decisions.md). Entries are in number order.

### D46. After the IK limb: a pose-driver with a marker spec, then the face module, and overlap only on request
**Status:** Leaning (author, 2026-11-13). Not built. Details: `module-directions.md`. The IK limb stays next (2026-11-10).
The next modules are a stateless pose-driver (cone, distance or RBF solves over world matrices, defined in JSON) with a marker spec for the engine, then the face module (D45) with a channel map and a connectivity rules file, and overlap only as a stateless approximation if animators ask. Stateful simulation stays engine-side.
- **Why (author's production experience):** secondary behaviour such as high collars and correctives is driven by pose and does not need state. Velocity-based overlap needs frame history, which breaks scrubbing and parallel evaluation (D36).
- **Rejected:** a stateful simulation module. A live node for face connectivity (the driven channel would be unkeyable, so rules are data applied by an animator-facing tool). Building the system around swappable gear parts: the plug and socket model limited design continuity and paid off mainly for microtransactions.
- **Consequence:** each module goes through the gate (D12). Open: whether the engine evaluates pose-driver definitions, and whether a marker is an export extension or a module.

### D52. Two-bone IK: a `TwoBoneIk` limb with a matched IK/FK switch and module-owned spaces
**Status:** Decided (author, 2026-11-15: three rounds of answers). All eight phases built 2026-11-15 (the forward module, the inverse, the matched switch `setRigIkFkBlend` and `setRigControlSpace`, the perf rig and `bench`). The gate's `bench` result is below target (D36: 27 and 22 fps for 20 rigs, 1.6 to 1.7 times the floor on 50): the limbs' cost is node count, not the solver (1.4% of node time), and the author accepted the cost (2026-11-15, D36). The rig panel's IK/FK controls are mayadev work, proposed and not built. Design and build phases: `two-bone-ik.md`.
The IK limb (2026-11-10) is a two-bone limb: exactly three joints, for arms and legs. One-bone IK is `Aim`, and three or more bones or spline IK would be their own modules.
- **Controls:** an FK chain whose start control also moves the IK root (translation only, its rotation does nothing to the IK chain), an IK handle, a pole and a channel-only settings control. The handle, pole and settings take their markup and rest from three non-export marker joints, selected in `moduleArgs` like Aim's `target` and `up`.
- **Channels:** `ikFkBlend` (default 1, IK, for every limb), `stretch`, `soft` and `pin` (0 to 1), `roll` on the handle (`mirrorNegate`), and a module-owned `space` on the pole (`Parent`, `Ik`). Soft IK is in v1. FK controls keep translate, rotate and scale so FK can reproduce a stretched or pinned pose. Controls hide by blend.
- **No pop:** the node publishes the control values that match each side to the current pose (`fkMatchLocal`, `ikMatchLocal`, pulled only by tools), and `setRigIkFkBlend` writes them and sets the blend. A raw `ikFkBlend` change doesn't match, like a raw `space` change (D27).
- **Module-owned spaces** are a general concept: a `space` enum a module reads itself, with static labels and the module's own `attributeAffects`, reported by `getRigControlSpace` (a new `module` key) and switched by `setRigControlSpace(control, ...)`, which matches either kind of space (D27), a module's through a `ModuleDescriptor::spaceDelta` hook. Named for the control because the space belongs to the control, not the module. The pole's is the first. `rigConstraint` can't give the pole an IK handle space, because the module's own socket as a driver is a cycle (D17).
- **The settings control** follows the `parentStructure` socket of the module's first child plug (the chest for an arm, the pelvis for a leg), through an internal child plug the template can't name, so a constraint on `start` doesn't carry it.
- **Inverse (amends D41, built 2026-11-15):** the contract gains scalar channels, a vector like its other fields: `ModuleDescriptor::inverseChannels` names them (channel, slot, default) and `ModuleInverseInput::channels` carries their values in internal units, filled from the sidecar or the rig's current value so the inverse uses the value the forward solve will. FkChain, Aim and Layout list none. The handle's distance is a bisection on the forward reach, and the pole a quadratic, so soft, stretch and pin are exact in any mix (`TwoBoneIkSolve::computeTwoBoneIkInverse`, moved from `MathUtils` on 2026-11-20). The match outputs will call the same inverse inside `compute()` and allocate, the one exception to the allocation-free rule, accepted because only tools pull them.
- **Rejected:** FK values as offsets from IK (FK poses stop transferring, and the other direction is a cycle), a `poleFollow` slider beside a constraint enum, relaxing D17 to per-slot dependencies, derived usages with a computed pole, a pole-only `setRigPoleSpace` and a `setRigModuleSpace`, a template-named settings child plug, settings following `start`'s constraint, a fixed-size channel array or contract (it would cap FkChain's length, and the matrices would allocate anyway).
- **Phase 4 settled:** the internal `settings` plug is `ModulePlugInfo::followsPlug` (pass 2 reads the followed plug's `parentStructure` entry), `createModule` runs the modifier twice to add and wire the channels (as `createConstraint` does), and the FK chain is solved at every blend so each control's offset stays current. Details in `two-bone-ik.md` section 8.
- **Phase 7 settled:** `ModuleDescriptor::spaceDelta` is a free function pointer like `inverse`, `(module, slot, label) -> delta`, and `TwoBoneIkNode::spaceDelta` reads the same two deltas `evaluate` gives the pole from the module's plugs (the child plug's parent motion, and the `ik` socket against its rest), so the command runs no solve. D27's formula has one home, `RigPoseScene::matchControlToNewDelta`, now built on `fitLocalToChannels` (the constraint's switch calls it too): a locked or driven channel warns only when its kept value differs from the match's, where it used to warn for every locked channel. A new `ControlSpaceScene` finds a control's space of either kind, so the two commands stay thin. `D_old` is the control's current enum value, not a stored one. `ControlSpace.module` is appended with a default in Python, so a caller that builds the dataclass by position keeps working.
- **Consequence:** one module node (`twoBoneIkModule`, `0x00218F`), six channel names, two commands (`setRigIkFkBlend`, `setRigControlSpace`), a `module` key on `getRigControlSpace`, `ModulePlugInfo::allowsConstraint`, a descriptor hook, and a mayadev rig panel edit. Built in eight phases, one commit each, with a `bench` run on the perf rig (D36).

### D53. Module source files and classes end in `Module`: `FkChainModule`

**Status:** Decided (author, 2026-11-15).

Layout, Aim and TwoBoneIk already had `<Name>Module.h/.cpp` and `<Name>ModuleNode`; FkChain alone was `FkChain.h/.cpp` and `FkChainNode`. It is now `FkChainModule.h/.cpp` and `FkChainModuleNode`, and every future module file follows the pattern.

- **Not renamed:** the template `moduleType` (`FkChain`, `Aim`, ...) and the node type name (`fkChainModule`). Both are public: saved templates and rigs depend on them, and none of them carries the suffix in `moduleType`.

- **Impact:** none on saved rigs, poses, animation or templates. Only C++ names and paths changed.

### D56. Ribbon module: a virtual ribbon with ik controls, tweakers and stateless twist
**Status:** Leaning (author, 2026-11-17, three rounds of answers). Built in part on 2026-11-17: phases 1, 2, 3 and 5 and the docs (the math, the node, the layers, the inverse). Not built: `fkStyle` and the perf rig with `bench`. Design: `ribbon.md`, with an "As built" section. Branch `ribbon-module-planning`.

A virtual (no curve, no mesh) parameterized ribbon for tails, spines and limb segments: n bind joints already distributed, j control joints (start, end, interior ones that ride the curve), a twist that runs the whole chain and one sine layer held on the first control, scale with a three-point volume profile, and an inverse in v1.
- **Why:** the handoff sketch's driver placement through `offsetParentMatrix` was a module output feeding its own controls (D17, D52), and its accumulated twist state made output depend on frame order (D36). Reading each interior control's local matrix removes the cycle without a node pair, and the branch-picking twist, `secondary + wrap(extracted - secondary)`, reaches past 180 degrees with no state and no keyed twist channel.
- **Decided:** a control layer stack (optional FK, IK, hidden tweakers as the bind joints' parents), `fkStyle` as a template key, start and end driven by another module's sockets or self-contained (an arm is two ribbons), `uniformSpacing` dropped (rest spacing kept), rest up vector from the start joint's rest axis, `volumePreserve` with start, mid and end weights, inverse in v1 with the tweakers as the exact residual.
- **Round three (2026-11-17):** reference space is the first child plug's parent, channels live on the first IK control, FK controls are a real chain read through their local matrices, v1 layers are twist, bow and one sine behind a per-layer function, `lambda` clamped for the volume exponent only, driven start and end visible and with no space match. See `ribbon.md` section 9.
- **Built (2026-11-17):** `core/RibbonMath` (the solve, no Maya types), `maya/modules/RibbonModule` (`ribbonModule`, MTypeId 0x002191, moduleType `Ribbon`), the channels on the first control (15 after the revision below), ik controls plus hidden tweakers, a `start` and an `end` child plug, an inverse that is exact for every bind joint present, and the suite (the contract, 12 hand-worked pose cases, behaviour, plan checks and a golden snapshot).
- **Differences from the draft** (all in `ribbon.md` "As built"): the module layer already fed every control's local matrix to its module, so no new plug type was needed. A bind joint's parameter is its share of the control polyline's rest length, not an arc-length table. An interior control's own roll twists the ribbon too. `volumePreserve` defaults to 0. The ribbon is solved in the rest world and carried by the `start` plug's parent motion, which is the draft's "parent of the first child plug" without inverting anything per control.
- **Revision (author, 2026-11-17, phase 2):** (1) **The ends' twist runs the whole chain.** The two ends' total twists (the branch-picked angles) ramp linearly in `u` along the ribbon, and every interior control's offset parent matrix carries that ramp about the base curve's tangent, so a roll on the start reaches the far end and not only the next control. An interior control's own roll adds on top of the ramp at its place (its total is ramp plus own), and the joints between controls follow the controls' totals linearly. A consequence animators will see: an interior control's local axes are rolled by the ramp, so its translate channels move it along the rolled axes. (2) **`twistFalloffPower` removed**: the roll between controls is linear, which the ramp makes the only sensible spread. (3) **`bow` removed**: the sine layer is the only nonlinear displacement left. `bendRoundness` stays: it sizes the end tangents and is not a layer. The channel count is 15, and the `RibbonChannel` order closed up (nothing was committed, so no saved pose, animation or golden depended on the old order). Rejected: keeping `twistFalloffPower` as a shaping curve over the ramp (a second way to spread twist, with no case that needed it).
- **Scale** (checked 2026-11-17): control scales interpolate by smoothstep between controls, and `volumePreserve` multiplies the Y and Z axes by `lambda^(-0.5 * volumePreserve * w(u))`. Hand-worked pose cases cover a stretch (lambda 1.5), a squash (lambda 0.5), half preservation, the profile weights, a control's own scale on its own, and a control's scale together with volume (they multiply).
- **Measured (2026-11-17, `bench` parallel, headless):** the first build measured the 50-rig ribbon rig at 308 ms against a 72 ms floor (ratio 4.3). **That number was a bug, not the ribbons' cost:** `compute()` read its stored rest arrays with `jumpToElement` and failed on a reopened scene, because a saved scene drops array elements that equal their default (knot 0, an identity offset) and an array with none has no handle. Every ribbon failed its compute every frame and printed an error. `compute()` now reads a missing element as its default (test: `test_a_saved_and_reopened_scene_evaluates_the_same`). **After the fix: 50 rigs 107 to 116 ms/frame (8.6 to 9.4 fps) against a floor of 66 ms (15 fps): ratio 1.6 to 1.7. 20 rigs 47 to 50 ms (20 to 21 fps).** The rig was 93 ms against 57 before the ribbons (1.6 to 1.7, D52), so the ribbons add no measurable ratio. Both are over D36's 1.5 and 30 fps targets, for the reason D36 gives (the `rigControlNode` premium over a native transform), and the ribbon is not the cause. Probes: `RibbonMath::evaluate` for 3 controls and 12 bind joints costs 5.5 microseconds, so 350 ribbons are about 2 ms of a frame, and the handoff's suspects (struct initialisation, plug copies, three curve evaluations per bind frame, `pow`/`sin`/`cos`, the matrix inverse) are not worth changing. Hidden tweakers cost nothing extra headless: 3000 controls un-hidden gave 111 to 112 ms against 113 to 122 for the same scene hidden (noise is about 5 ms).
- **Amendment (2026-11-17, phase 1 of `ribbon-twist-follow.md`):** the end's total twist also includes the twist of its plug's motion relative to the start plug's (`RibbonMath::Input::endDelta`, the swing-twist split about the end's rest X), so an end parent that twists (an elbow, a chest) twists the ribbon, and an arm's two ribbons meet at the elbow with the shoulder's twist easing in. Before it, the end plug's roll was ignored: the probe measured a seam pop equal to the shoulder's twist (60 and 170 degrees), and a start parented to the shoulder gave no ease. A Twist constraint type was rejected: a plug delta carries the whole ribbon rigidly, about the rest axis. No name changes, so poses and animation are unaffected. A rig with a twisting end parent looks different; no rigs need to survive. **Correction (2026-11-17, found on the solomon asset):** the first build of this term read the twist of the plug motion about the world X. The plug deltas are motions in world axes, so the module now conjugates the end's motion by the end control's rest rotation and reads the twist about the control's own X, the bone's axis (commit `ea4b0d3` on main had the world-X version, right only for a bone along X). `test_a_tilted_arm_follows_its_end_plugs_twist_about_the_bone` covers it. Phase 2 adds optional `startAimBlend` and `endAimBlend` `moduleArgs` keys (0 to 1, default 0): the same-named channels' default, so their rest (D34), because a template had no way to build a shoulder or thigh aimed. Rejected: changing the channels' own default to 1 (it would move every Ribbon's rest, tails included) and leaving it to the animator (lost on a zero or a pose load). The two keys are public API.
- **Open:** `fkStyle` (not built), a GUI look at the control shapes, and a read-phase warning for control joints whose X points away down the chain.
- **Consequence:** the channel names (`startTwist`, `endTwist`, `sineAmplitude`, `sineFrequency`, `sinePhase`, `sineOrient`, `sineStartFalloff`, `sineEndFalloff`, `startAimBlend`, `endAimBlend`, `bendRoundness`, `volumePreserve`, `scaleStart`, `scaleMid`, `scaleEnd`), the `controls` and `joints` moduleArgs keys, and the child plug and socket names are public API now. `ModuleInverseContext` gained `ikControlCount`, which `RigInverse` fills from a module's `ikControlCount` attribute.

### D57. Control placement is derived from the bind joints, with no control joints in the skeleton
**Status:** Decided (author, 2026-11-17). Phases 0 to 5 built (the -X chain, `core/ControlPlacement`, the Ribbon, TwoBoneIk, the perf scenes, the docs pass and the mayadev report). Design and build phases: `derived-controls.md`.

The Ribbon places its ik controls evenly along its bind joints (`controlCount`, default 3), and TwoBoneIk derives its IK handle, pole and settings from its three joints, so neither needs control joints in the skeleton. Orientation comes from the bind joints, and a joint whose axis is off the chain is a read-phase error. A chain whose -X points down is accepted (mirrored limbs). Control names are a base plus `IK`, `Tweak` or `FK` (`spineIK_00`, `armIK`), the base set by an optional `controlName`. The pole and settings take optional world-space `poleOffset` and `settingsOffset` vectors (the settings default is a quarter of the limb's length along world +Y, and a straight limb puts the pole out along the root joint's -Y rather than failing). Ribbon placement follows a smooth spline through the bind joints, with the control cap at 8. The foot pivots (`heel`, `bankIn`, `bankOut`) stay marker joints.
- **Why:** 35 of the 95 joints in a real skeleton were `skin: false`, all exported, and 20 of them carried no information beyond the bind joints. Each one also changed the skeleton hash. **Rejected:** a locator input type with a `markers` array in `skeleton.json` (a new node type, new tools, a hash decision to reopen), and keeping marker joints with `export: false` (fixes only the engine side).
- **Reverses:** D52 decision 1 (marker joints for `ik`, `pole` and `settings`, and its rejected computed pole) and D56's `controls` selectors in `moduleArgs`. D54's hash rule stands.
- **Built, phase 2 (2026-11-18):** the Ribbon's `moduleArgs` are `joints`, `controlCount` and `controlName`, `moduleVersion` 2 turns an old `controls` list into `controlCount`, and the ik controls have no joint (`wireJoint` is skipped for them). Usages are `<base>IK_<nn>` and `<base>Tweak_<nn>`. A tweaker's usage is no longer its bind joint's, though its node is still named from the joint. The inverse takes the end controls from the first and last bind joints and leaves interior controls at their sidecar or rest values, the tweakers carrying the difference. A usage two modules share is the existing D25 error.
- **Built, phase 3 (2026-11-18):** TwoBoneIk's `moduleArgs` are `joints`, `controlName`, `poleOffset` and `settingsOffset`. `moduleVersion` 2 drops `ik`, `pole` and `settings`, and cannot turn the old marker positions into offsets (the update step has no scene), so an old template builds with the derived places and its pole and settings controls move (author, 2026-11-18: accepted). The handle sits at the end joint with its rotation. The pole and settings controls take **world axes**: their rotation is locked, so their orientation only sets how their translate channels read, and world axes are the least surprising. The offsets are world vectors with X negated on a mirrored copy, through `isMirror()` in `planFromJson`. A straight limb (mid within 0.1 percent of the limb's length of the root-to-end line) puts the pole out along the root joint's -Y (author, 2026-11-18: -Y is right, not -Z). A straight limb that runs along that same axis puts the default pole on the root-to-end line, so the read phase rejects it and asks for a `poleOffset`: a pole on that line has no bend plane. Usages are `<base>IK`, `<base>Pole` and `<base>Settings`, the base being `controlName`, else the first joint's usage without a trailing `_NN`. The IK, pole and settings slots have no joint (`wireJoint` is skipped, no `jointOPM` element). The inverse is unchanged: it never read the marker joints. `MathUtils::toMat4` and `toMMatrix` moved to the shared layer from the Ribbon.
- **Built, phase 4 (2026-11-18):** the perf generator writes `moduleVersion` 2 with `controlName`, `controlCount`, `poleOffset` and `settingsOffset` and no limb marker or ribbon control joints (296 joints, was 354; Aim keeps its markers). `derived_controls()` lists the 58 controls with no joint, and the floor scene builds them as native transforms, so both scenes keep 348 controls. `bench` (parallel, once): `complex_x20` 23.0 fps (was 21.8), `complex_x50` 9.3 fps against the floor's 16.1, a ratio of 1.74 (was 1.72). Both are still below D36's target (30 fps for 20 rigs, 1.5 times the floor on 50), as before: removing the joints did not change the cost.
- **Built, phase 5 (2026-11-18):** a docs verification pass only, no code. `docs/modules.md`, `controls.md`, `templates.md`, `skeletons.md`, `architecture.md`, `commands.md` and `README.md` already described the derived controls (marker joints remain only for Aim's `target` and `up`, and the foot pivots), with no stale `ik`, `pole`, `settings` or `controls` keys in a current example. `CLAUDE.md` now says the Ribbon's controls are derived. The three open questions in `derived-controls.md` section 9 are closed. mayadev call sites were searched and reported to the author, not edited. No suite rerun (no test or fixture changed).
- **Amended 2026-11-18 (author):** the settings control's default offset is a quarter of the limb's length along world +X, not +Y. On the mirrored side it is -X (`placeLimb` takes `mirrored`, passed from `planFromJson`), so the two sides reflect. An explicit `settingsOffset` is unchanged. Saved poses are unaffected (the controls' rest places move, their channel names do not).
- **Consequence:** the `controls` key leaves Ribbon's `moduleArgs` and `ik`, `pole` and `settings` leave TwoBoneIk's (both `moduleVersion` steps). Control usages change, so saved poses and animation for them stop matching. Open questions are in `derived-controls.md` section 9.

### D59. TwoBoneIk and Ribbon template keys for defaults, orientation and an FK layer
**Status:** Built (author, 2026-11-18). Built in steps on branch `claude/ribbon-two-bone-ik-updates-d3291d`, one commit each.
- **Step 1, built:** TwoBoneIk takes `ikFkBlend` (0 to 1, default 1) in `moduleArgs`. It is the built value and the rest value of the settings control's `ikFkBlend` channel, so an arm builds in FK and `zeroRigControls` returns to FK. Additive, so no `moduleVersion` step. Rejected: a separate `defaultMode` string, since the channel is already the blend.
- **Step 2, built:** `ikOrientToWorld` (the IK handle's rest frame is world-aligned) and `ikAlignToPole` (a yaw about world Y so +Z points at the pole's horizontal direction, for feet). Two flags because an arm's pole sits behind the elbow, so one flag would turn an arm's handle 180 degrees. `ikAlignToPole` without `ikOrientToWorld` is an error, not a silent no-op, and so is a pole straight above or below the end joint. The math is `ControlPlacement::placeWorldIkHandle` in `core/`. The joints' rest offsets (`computeRest`) absorb the handle's rotation, so the bind pose is unchanged. Mirror: the yaw follows the reflected pole. A world-aligned right handle has the left's axes, so its translate channels read differently from a joint-aligned one; the channels' `mirrorCopy` or `mirrorNegate` modes are unchanged.
- **Step 3, built:** Ribbon `fkStyle` (D56 phase 4) with `fkControlCount`, defaulting to `controlCount`. The chain has its own size, so the D56 line "each IK control takes its frame from the FK control at the same index" became "from the fk base curve at the ik control's own parameter", with the rest offset measured there (see `ribbon.md` section 3.1). The fk controls are the last slots after the tweakers, with no joint and no socket. With an fk chain the ribbon has no `end` child plug: the whole ribbon follows `start` and the end rides the chain. Rejected: keeping the `end` plug and letting it twist the ribbon as before, since the fk chain's end already is the end's motion and two drivers of one end is the kind of surprise animators hit. No new perf-rig instances, since this extends an existing module. `bench` (2026-11-18, one run each, parallel): complex x20 16.5 fps (60.6 ms), the same scene with every ribbon given an fk chain (`complex --fk-style`) 16.1 fps (61.9 ms), so the chain costs about 1.3 ms for 20 rigs, within run-to-run noise. Complex x50 147.7 ms against the floor's 91.1 ms is 1.62 times. Both are below the D36 targets (30 fps for 20 rigs, 1.5 times the floor), and there is no baseline run from before D59 in this session, so whether steps 1 and 2 moved them is not measured (D56 recorded 20 to 21 fps and 107 to 116 ms against a 66 ms floor on an earlier run).

### D62. Limb: one module that runs the TwoBoneIk and Ribbon solves with one continuous ribbon

**Status:** Decided (author, 2026-11-19: one round of answers). Built through phase 3. Design, interface and phases: `limb.md`.

An arm or leg built from `TwoBoneIk`, two or three `Ribbon` modules and `Swing` and `Twist` constraints has a crease at the elbow, 21 controls, five nodes and the ribbon channels repeated on two controls (checked on the solomon rig). `Limb` is one module with one spline through start, mid and end.

- **Inputs:** three IK/FK joints and 5 or more bind joints (they may have child joints: inheritsTransform is off, so a child is not dragged; author, 2026-11-22), with exactly one at the mid joint and none stacked. `controlCount` ribbon ik controls, default 5, minimum 3. No fk ribbon.
- **Channels:** every module-wide channel (`ikFkBlend`, `stretch`, `soft` and the ribbon's) is on the settings control, under the names already in use. `startAimBlend` and `endAimBlend` are dropped. `roll` and `pin` stay on the handle and the pole.
- **Evaluation:** the IK/FK solve, then the ribbon's end frames from the blended joints (start: translation and swing of the start joint, as `Swing`; end: translation and twist of the end joint on the swing of the mid, as `Twist`), then the ribbon solve. No constraint nodes.
- **DAG parenting (author):** the ribbon ik controls are leaf children of the start plug's DAG parent, and the tweakers are leaf children of the first ik control.
- **Reuse, and the coupling:** `Limb` is not a subclass of `TwoBoneIk` or `Ribbon`. It calls their solves, which phase 0 moves into shared functions with no behaviour change, and their channel specs, control derivation and inverses. **A change to those, or to `core/RibbonMath` or `core/SwingTwist`, changes `Limb`.** The warning is in `limb.md`, `two-bone-ik.md`, `ribbon.md`, `CLAUDE.md` and three headers, and `test_limb_matches_parts` checks that the three agree.
- **Hand and foot:** one `Limb`, not `Arm` and `Leg`. An optional `ikTarget` child plug sets where the end joint reaches, defaulting to the handle's own world. A later `ReverseFoot` module hangs from the `ik` socket and feeds `ikTarget`, so the foot pushes up the ankle target while the handle stays the foot's root. It makes a module-level loop that is acyclic at plug level, so the cycle check in D17 gets a declared exemption for that plug (author-approved). Rejected: a foot inside `Limb`, and a combined `Limb` and `ReverseFoot`. How an IK match works with a raised foot is left to the `ReverseFoot` design.
- **Names:** type `Limb`, node `limbModule`, MTypeId `0x002192`, controls `<base>FK`, `<base>IK`, `<base>Pole`, `<base>Settings`, `<base>RibbonIK_<nn>`, `<base>Tweak_<nn>`.
- **Migration:** none. No legacy rig has to survive (author), and the solomon template moves to `Limb` by hand in phase 6.
- **Phase 1, built (2026-11-19):** the curve through the mid joint is D59's fk base curve with the limb's three blended joint frames as the chain (`RibbonMath::buildAnchoredSetup`, `Setup::fkAnchors`, `Input::fkWorld`), so there is no new curve type. Two changes beyond the anchors: the anchors' roll about the curve also twists the bind joints, and the ik controls are placed on the anchor curve from chosen parameters (the mid one at the mid joint's), which amends D57 for `Limb`. The elbow is rounded, not creased, and no rounder than the Catmull-Rom tangent at the mid knot gives (46 and 34 degrees in the two steps at a 98 degree bend with four bind joints a side). A mid-handle scale is the open choice (`limb.md` section 5.1). Verified by a standalone probe over hand-worked cases (34 checks) that become `Limb` pose cases. The old `Ribbon` is unchanged.
- **Phase 2, built (2026-11-19):** the `Limb` module (`limbModule`, `0x002192`). The two-bone half is shared as `TwoBoneIkPart<Node>` (attributes, plug reads, slots, channels) and `TwoBoneIkSolve::planSlots`, and the ribbon's channels, stored rest and evaluation as `RibbonSolve`, so `TwoBoneIk` and `Ribbon` now run on the code `Limb` uses. **Amended by the author (2026-11-19): the elbow is a sharp corner by default.** `bendSoftness` (0 to 1, rest 0, on the settings control) blends from the polyline through the joints to the smooth curve, in both the base curve and the detail curve, and replaces `bendRoundness` (which, with the aim blends, is not a Limb channel). A curve degree was rejected: a cubic with chord tangents is exactly a line, so one channel spans both. A split spline needed its own frame transport (the normal flips across a corner over a quarter turn) and a roll measured on each side of a corner. The test of the work: four hand-worked pose cases, eight validation cases, the contract (its three inverse tests skipped until phase 3), DAG parenting, and `test_limb_matches_parts`. Not built: the inverse, the matched switch for a Limb (`setRigIkFkBlend` is TwoBoneIk's), `ikTarget` and the perf-rig instances.

### D71. FkHierarchy: a separate module that builds an FK control for every joint under one root
**Status:** Decided (signed off by the author 2026-11-23) and built the same day on branch `fk-hierarchy`. Design, and where the build differs from it (section 16): `design/fk-hierarchy.md`.

A new module, `FkHierarchy`, takes a `root` selector and an optional `terminators` list, and builds one FK control for the root and for every joint below it, down to each terminator (included) or to every leaf when there are none. One child plug, `start` (the root). Sockets are named from joint markup: the side lowercased, then the usage (`left_spine_00`), with `start` also naming the root's. FkChain is unchanged.

- **Why a new module, not an FkChain extension:** FkChain's interface is positional (an ordered `joints` list, `index_<n>` sockets, `start` and `end` aliases), its names are public API, and its compute is a straight loop on the most-used module. A tree changes all three. The two share control creation, wiring, `solveControlStep` and the inverse step. Rejected: a mode on FkChain, which puts two interface shapes behind one `moduleType`.
- **Compute stays one linear pass:** controls are stored in pre-order, a `controlParentIndex` int array gives each control's parent, and a delta is kept only for a control that has children. Splitting the tree into chains was rejected (the same cross-chain lookup with more bookkeeping). A separate `solveFkHierarchy` rather than a flag on `solveFkChain`, so FkChain's loop, goldens and D36 numbers cannot move.
- **Author's rules (2026-11-23):** one root per module (a second root is a second module); every joint and control has unique markup (D25); a joint added under the root builds and its control inherits its parent; the joints are found with `MItDag`; it mirrors (D28), and a mirrored terminator that matches no joint on the other side is dropped with a warning, while a mirror that needs specific terminators is written out as its own module.
- **Also decided (2026-11-23):** a joint under the root that another module claims is a template error, avoided with a terminator, and there is no `exclude` list (a second `FkHierarchy` below it covers that); the side is lowercased and the usage keeps its case; `start` is the root's alias and there is no `end`; controls are in depth-first `MItDag` order, not sorted; the ordering rule is in `core/` and the walk in the Maya layer. 
- **Built (2026-11-23):** `core/HierarchyPlan`, `FkHierarchyModule`, the inverse with `ModuleInverseContext::parentIndex`, and a mirror rule the draft missed: `TemplateMirror` swaps a leading `left_` or `right_` on a socket name, so a mirrored module on a markup-named socket points at the other side's. Suite on that build: TOTAL 1562 passed, 15 skipped.
- **Perf rig and `bench` (once, at creation, D36):** the complex rig gained 4 `hierarchy_specs` (6 modules built, 8 joints each): 408 joints, 524 controls, 103 constraints. `bench` parallel, one session: 20 rigs 13.8 fps (72.7 ms); 50 rigs 5.5 fps (181.8 ms) against a native floor of 8.9 fps (112.7 ms), a ratio of 1.61. **Both are below D36's target** (30 fps for 20 rigs, 1.5 times the floor on 50). The Limb entry's last run (360 joints, 476 controls) was 15.7 to 16.4 fps and a ratio of 1.74, so the 48 joints and controls added here are within the run-to-run drift. The cost is still node and control count, as in D52.
- **Consequence:** a new module name and node type (`fkHierarchyModule`, MTypeId `0x002193`), one attribute, one optional field on the inverse context, and the gate steps: perf rig instances and one `bench` run when it is built.

### D74. ReverseFoot: a foot module on a Limb, with roll, pivot and toe channels, an FK toe, a blend driven by the limb, and an inverse that solves three channels
**Status:** Decided (author's answers recorded, the seven open questions answered and signed off, 2026-11-24; `reverse-foot.md`). Phase 2 (`core/FootSolve`) and phase 3 (stage 1: the module, the control and channels, the plugs and the `ankle` socket) built 2026-11-24. Stage 2, `ikFkDriver` and the inverse are not. Needs D73. Design, interface, solve, inverse and phases: `reverse-foot.md`.
A separate module that hangs from a Limb (D62) by two child plugs: `ik` (the IK half, from the Limb's `ik` socket, outputs the `ankle` socket that feeds `ikTarget`) and `ankle` (from the Limb's `end` socket, the ankle as it is, for the toe and toe tip). Inputs are `joints` (ankle, toe, toe tip), `reverseHierarchy` (marker selectors in chain order, role from the usage: heel, ball, bankOut, bankIn, toeTip, toe) and `ikFkDriver` (the Limb or TwoBoneIk whose `ikFkBlend` drives the foot through a locked input). Channels only, on one channel control (`<base>Foot`, derived from the `ball` marker and aligned to the world, author 2026-11-24; the IK leg control would need a cross-module channel): `footRoll` (peel, split in the node at `breakAngle`, no set-driven keys), the raises and swivels of each pivot, `ballRoll`, one signed `bank` (positive toward world +X, so "out" on a left foot and "in" on a right foot, `mirrorNegate`) and `toeTap`. The only new FK control is the toe's: the ankle comes from the Limb's IK or FK end. The foot drives the toe and toe tip, never the ankle, which the Limb owns. `reverseHierarchy` has no ankle entry.
- **No cycle:** two stages in separate attribute groups (D62 phase 4: Maya orders by attribute). Stage 1 reads `ik`, stage 2 reads `end`, and the cycle check gets one more blind-socket rule.
- **Inverse:** the handle absorbs the rigid foot offset, so the ankle and toe come back exactly for any channels. Joint-solved: `footRoll` (the ankle's total pitch), `bank` (its roll) and `toeTap`. Not solvable: the raises, swivels and `ballRoll`, which come from the sidecar or the rig. `RigInverse` gets a pre-pass that solves the foot first and gives the Limb the offset (`ModuleInverseContext::ikTargetOffset`), so the match through `setRigIkFkBlend` does not pop. This closes the open item in D62.
- **Rejected:** `Leg` and `Arm` modules, and a foot inside `Limb` (D62, and the author 2026-11-24: no `Leg`). FK pivot controls as a second interface (two drivers of one state, D59 step 3). The foot owning an `ikFkBlend` of its own. One plug on `ik` only (the foot would not follow the FK ankle).
- **Phase 2 built (2026-11-24): `core/FootSolve`, and how the chain composes (author's answer).** The design's `pivot_i = pivot_{i-1} * rotation_i * restOffset_i` is the **literal hierarchy with the heel as root**: a pivot is carried by the pivots before it. Because a pivot's own turn about its own position does not move it, that is the same as `ankle' = Rot_0(Rot_1(... Rot_n(ankle)))`, each about a REST pivot and a rest axis of the foot frame, the last pivot (the toe tip) moving the ankle first. The author was shown the alternative (every rotation about fixed ground pivots, applied in list order) and chose the hierarchy knowing what it does past the break angle: with heel z -10, ball z 10, toe tip z 18 and the ankle at (y 8, z -6), a roll of 50 over a break of 30 puts the ankle at (y 19.53, z 7.63) and carries the toe tip 4 below the ground (the alternative gave y 20.14, z 6.33 with the tip on the ground). Signs, all positive about the axis (right-handed): a ball or toe tip pitch is about lateral (heel up), a heel pivot's angle is minus that (toes up), swivels are about up (toes toward lateral), `ballRoll` and `bank` are minus a turn about forward, and the bank turns about the pivot on the +lateral side for a positive value. Within one pivot the roll turns first, then the pitch, then the swivel, the order `decomposeRotation` reads back. Where a roll sits between two pitches (bank between the ball and the toe tip, past the break), the pitch and roll the inverse reads are not the forward `footRoll` and `bank`: the handle absorbs the difference, so the ankle and toe still come back exactly.
- **Phase 3 built (2026-11-24): stage 1, and the decisions on it (author).** (1) The `ik` and `ankle` child plugs have **no control and allow no constraint**, as Limb's `ikTarget` has none: a template constraint on either is a read-phase error. The design's "a constraint on either works as for other plugs" would need a control per plug to host the space attribute, which the foot has not got. (2) `controlName` is the whole usage of the channel control (default `foot`, so `foot_00_L_CTRL`), not a base that `Foot` is added to. (3) `ReverseFootModuleNode` is `MTypeId` 0x002194. The `ik` plug is `childPlugs[0]`, like every plug. The `ankle` plug is three inputs of its own (`anklePlugMatrix`, `anklePlugRestMatrix`, `anklePlugConnected`) and the `ankle` socket an output array of its own (`ankleSocketMatrix`, found by `getSocketMatrixPlug`), so the two stages are on separate attributes and the `ankle` plug has `blindSockets` `{ankle}`. (4) The solve is one `compute()` for the control's OPM and the ankle socket: the socket is `ankleRest * footMotion * handleDelta`, the foot's pivots turning the ankle first and the handle's motion since rest carrying it. (5) The module **claims the toe and toe tip and does not drive them**: createModule only freezes each at its rest (inheritsTransform off, the rest baked into its offset parent matrix) so a rig built now keeps them where they are. The ankle is only read (`findJointRest`), the Limb drives it. `ikFkDriver` is required, stored in an attribute and checked for being given: its existence, type and the blend-source `ModuleInterface` method are phase 4. (6) Marker resolution is built for modules: `PlanContext::markers` and `RigModuleNodeBase::resolveMarker`, with every error at once naming the marker and the module (no file passed, no such marker, joint not among the build's). (7) `addAngleChannel` gained an optional rest, so `breakAngle` rests at 30. The ModuleContract's inverse round trips are skipped for this module until phase 5, and its one-solve test is its own (the control's offset and the ankle socket in one compute).
- **Note (author, 2026-11-24):** with the foot reading the Limb's blend, sockets and handle, Limb and ReverseFoot are now a coupled pair, which may become a reason to separate Leg and Arm logic. Not now. A change to Limb's blend, `ikTarget` or inverse changes the foot.
- **Consequence:** a module and node, `core/FootSolve`, 11 channels and 2 controls, a cross-module blend source (`ikFkDriver`, new), an inverse pre-pass, perf-rig instances and one `bench` at creation (D36).
